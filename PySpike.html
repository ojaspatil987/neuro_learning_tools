SPIKE-distance

How different are two spike trains, moment by moment?

Two neurons. The curve below says how out of sync they are at each instant. The average height of the curve is the SPIKE-distance.

A spike train is a list of times

A spike has no amplitude and no shape. All it has is when.

Drag a spike · click empty space to add one · double-click (or drag off the row) to delete

We want a number that is 0 for identical trains and grows as they disagree, with no bin width and no time constant. We also want it time-resolved, a curve \(S(t)\) rather than a single number.

Stand at one instant

Pick a time \(t\). In each train, \(t\) falls between a previous spike and a following one.

$$t_\mathrm{P}^{(n)}=\max_i\{t_i^{(n)}\le t\},\qquad t_\mathrm{F}^{(n)}=\min_i\{t_i^{(n)}>t\},\qquad x_\mathrm{ISI}^{(n)}=t_\mathrm{F}^{(n)}-t_\mathrm{P}^{(n)}$$

Drag anywhere (outside the spikes) to move \(t\).

So every instant is boxed in by four corner spikes. The dashed ticks at \(0\) and \(T\) are auxiliary spikes, added so that the first and last intervals exist.

How far is each corner from the other train?

For each corner spike, find the nearest spike in the other train.

$$\Delta t_\mathrm{P}^{(1)}=\min_i\,\big|\,t_\mathrm{P}^{(1)}-t_i^{(2)}\big|$$

That gives four mismatches. They belong to the spikes, not to \(t\), so they stay fixed across the whole interval.

The nearer corner gets the bigger vote

$$S_1(t)=\frac{\Delta t_\mathrm{P}^{(1)}\,x_\mathrm{F}^{(1)}+\Delta t_\mathrm{F}^{(1)}\,x_\mathrm{P}^{(1)}}{x_\mathrm{ISI}^{(1)}},\qquad x_\mathrm{P}=t-t_\mathrm{P},\;\; x_\mathrm{F}=t_\mathrm{F}-t$$

This is just linear interpolation: \(S_1=\Delta t_\mathrm{P}\) at the previous spike and \(S_1=\Delta t_\mathrm{F}\) at the next. Dot size shows each corner's weight.

\(S_1(t)\)\(S_2(t)\)

These are still in units of time. A 5 ms mismatch matters if spikes are 10 ms apart, and hardly at all if they are 1 s apart.

Divide out the time scale

$$S(t)=\frac{S_1\,x_\mathrm{ISI}^{(2)}+S_2\,x_\mathrm{ISI}^{(1)}}{2\,\big\langle x_\mathrm{ISI}^{(n)}\big\rangle_n^{2}}$$

Each train's mismatch is weighted by the other train's interval, then divided by the squared mean interval. The result is dimensionless, lies in \([0,1]\), and is piecewise linear. It is zero only where the spikes coincide.

\(S(t)\)\(S'(t)=\dfrac{S_1+S_2}{2\langle x_\mathrm{ISI}\rangle}\) (no cross-weighting)

Average it

$$D_S=\frac1T\int_0^T S(t)\,dt$$

Now copy a train and jitter every spike by \(\sigma\), measured in units of the mean ISI.

Drag on this plot to set \(\sigma\). The line is the mean over 30 random trains. The dot is the pair on the left.

Spike times only enter through ratios, so rescaling time, \(t\to\lambda t\), leaves \(S(t)\) unchanged. There is nothing to tune.

Playground

Try to predict the curve before you let go.

More than two neurons

$$S^a(t)=\frac{2}{N(N-1)}\sum_{n<m}S^{nm}(t),\qquad D_S^a=\frac1T\int_0^T S^a(t)\,dt$$

Average over every pair. Because you can freeze any instant, you also get the full pairwise matrix: who is synchronous with whom, right now.

No peeking at the future

\(S(t)\) needs the following spikes, so you only know it after the fact. For online use, keep only past information:

$$S_r(t)=\frac{\Delta t_\mathrm{P}^{(1)}+\Delta t_\mathrm{P}^{(2)}}{4\,\big\langle x_\mathrm{P}^{(n)}\big\rangle_n},\qquad \text{(nearest spikes searched only among } t_i\le t)$$
\(S_r(t)\)smoothed \(S_r\)\(S(t)\) (needs the future)

\(S_r\) peaks just after a spike: one neuron has fired and the other hasn't yet. Only later spikes can resolve that uncertainty. A causal moving average smooths the peaks out.
